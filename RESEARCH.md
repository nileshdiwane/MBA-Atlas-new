# Research brief: what the application reproduces

Research snapshot: 14 November 2026. This product is an independent educational simulation. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or accredited by Harvard University or any other school named below.

## Evidence-backed design decisions

| School / source | Publicly documented programme pattern | Implemented interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Harvard Business School | A common first-year Required Curriculum plus FIELD; a summer work experience; a second-year Elective Curriculum. The 2026–27 RC lists Finance I, Financial Reporting and Control, Leadership and Organizational Behavior, Marketing, Technology and Operations Management, Strategy, Data Science and AI for Leaders, Business, Government and the International Economy, The Entrepreneurial Manager, Finance II, Leadership and Corporate Accountability, and Purpose of the Firm. Students may choose up to five EC courses a semester. | A two-year sequence with a common general-management core, required internship, two elective terms, and capstone. Course names are factual references; all lessons, cases, rubrics, and assessments in this repository are original unless a linked source says otherwise. |
| HBS Case Method | HBS describes four steps: read/analyse, discuss in a small group, engage in faculty-facilitated class discussion, reflect. It states that students read about 500 cases over two years. | Every case has an individual memo, study-group position, cold call, board plan, debate, and reflection. The volume is intentionally smaller than HBS and is labelled as a simulation. |
| HBS grading | HBS publicly describes Category I–IV/X grading and says typical course grades may combine 30–50% participation, about 5–15% midterm and 30–60% final. Exact grading varies by course. | The simulation uses transparent percentage grades, not Harvard categories. Default case-course weights are 35% participation, 15% applied work, 15% midterm, 35% final. This is inspired by the public range but is not HBS grading. |
| Stanford GSB | First-year analytical, leadership and management foundations; calibrated course levels; Leadership Labs in five-to-six-person squads; second year primarily electives. | Diagnostic pre-work, mastery bands, leadership squad exercises, peer feedback, role play, and a flexible elective year. |
| Wharton | 19 credit units: 9 core, 5 major, 5 electives; fixed and flexible core; 22 concentrations and nearly 200 courses are advertised. | Specialization pathways combine required and optional electives; degree audit visibly separates common core, pathway depth and free electives. Credit values are simulation credits, not Wharton CUs. |
| INSEAD | One year, five eight-week periods; 14 cores, 10.5 electives, a capstone; each period concludes with an exam, essay and/or project. | Eight-week intensive option, period-end assessment pattern, global cohort prompts and a strategy simulation capstone. |
| MIT Sloan | Six required first-semester core courses, one core elective, optional proseminar; small teams in cohorts; three later semesters with 144 elective units and hands-on learning. MIT OpenCourseWare publishes five required core courses and three core electives. | Data-driven core, rotating study teams, labs, simulations and a research library that links to legal open courseware without copying it. |
| Chicago Booth / Columbia | Booth documents a highly flexible curriculum built on foundations and LEAD; Columbia documents four terms, 18 core and 42 elective credits. | Elective planning supports a flexible path while the degree audit enforces breadth, residency-like engagement and minimum graded work. |

## Content and copyright policy

1. Official-school pages are used as factual programme-design references and are always identified as official links.
2. Harvard Business Publishing cases are not copied, summarized into substitutes, or sent to an AI provider. The library links to the official catalogue and tells the learner when purchase or institutional access is required.
3. Embedded case narratives in this application are original fictional simulations. Public-company exercises use learner-supplied or publicly available filings and include source prompts rather than copied proprietary case text.
4. MIT OpenCourseWare and OpenStax items are linked as supplementary open resources. Their own licence and attribution terms govern use.
5. School names and course titles appear only to describe factual research inputs. The interface uses independent branding and no school logos, seals, colour systems, or claims of equivalence.

## Product boundary

The application reproduces pedagogical patterns—preparation, discussion, cold calls, team work, exams, applied projects, reflection and progression. It does not reproduce any school's admissions, enrolled cohort, faculty, copyrighted cases, confidential teaching notes, official grades, transcript, degree, network or accreditation.
